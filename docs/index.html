1 Research question

What is the smallest sample size (n) at which I would be comfortable calling the sampling distribution of the sample mean approximately normal? This project treats that as an evidence-based judgment, not a universal theorem with one answer.

2 Is (n=30) a universal rule?

No. The generated evidence shows that n = 30 is context-dependent: it is enough for the exactly Normal benchmark and already persuasive for the synthetic age mixture, but it leaves visible skewness or Q-Q deviations for the Exponential, small-probability Binomial, and non-identical Bernoulli cases. Cauchy and the dependent failure process do not receive a defensible finite selection under the tested criteria.

4 What the results say

The fastest cases were Standard Normal (exactly normal for every n) and the synthetic age-at-death mixture. The die was also close to normal by n = 100. The slower finite-variance cases were the Exponential and Binomial(size = 5, p = 0.10), where strong right skew and rare-event discreteness required n = 1,000 in this study. The non-identical Bernoulli process used the correct p_i-specific variance and reached its most persuasive tested evidence at n = 1,000.

The most important exception was Cauchy: even at n = 5,000, Q-Q RMSE stayed around 1.3 with extreme skewness and kurtosis, and the running mean remained sensitive to outliers. The dependent failure process improved as n grew but still showed positive skewness and Q-Q error above the Normal calibration through n = 1,000; its covariance structure means the iid SE formula is not appropriate.
