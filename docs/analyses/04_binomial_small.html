1 Research Question

Does the CLT behave differently when each observation is a small, highly discrete Binomial count with a low success probability?

2 Population / Process and Theory

Y_i ~ Binomial(size = 5, p = 0.10). The Binomial parameter size = 5 is not the CLT sample size n. Here E[Y_i] = 0.5, Var(Y_i) = 0.45, and the iid theoretical SE is sqrt(0.45) / sqrt(n).

3 CLT Assumptions and Prediction

The draws are iid with finite variance, so the ordinary CLT applies. The low mean and discreteness may keep the sampling distribution lumpy and right-skewed at modest n.

4 Simulation Design and Evidence

The runner uses B = 10,000, a broad grid, and reviewed refinements. The display uses the largest generated n only to make the report useful after a run.

## Research Question

Does the CLT behave differently when each observation is a small, highly discrete Binomial count with a low success probability?

## Population / Process and Theory

`Y_i ~ Binomial(size = 5, p = 0.10)`. The Binomial parameter `size = 5` is not the CLT sample size `n`. Here `E[Y_i] = 0.5`, `Var(Y_i) = 0.45`, and the iid theoretical SE is `sqrt(0.45) / sqrt(n)`.

## CLT Assumptions and Prediction

The draws are iid with finite variance, so the ordinary CLT applies. The low mean and discreteness may keep the sampling distribution lumpy and right-skewed at modest `n`.

## Simulation Design and Evidence

The runner uses `B = 10,000`, a broad grid, and reviewed refinements. The display uses the largest generated `n` only to make the report useful after a run.
