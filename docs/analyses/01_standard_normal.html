spec <- population_spec("standard_normal"); rows <- population_rows(spec$id)

1 Research Question

How well do simulation diagnostics behave when the population is already Normal?

2 Population / Process and Theory

X_i ~ N(0, 1). Because Normal distributions are closed under averaging, the sample mean is exactly N(0, 1/n) for every positive integer n, including n = 1.

3 CLT Assumptions and Prediction

The observations are independent, identically distributed, and have finite variance. I expect all diagnostics to fluctuate around their theoretical targets at every tested n; those fluctuations quantify Monte Carlo noise.

4 Simulation Design, Search, and Evidence

The standard runner uses B = 10,000, the shared coarse grid, and any reviewed refinement values entered in R/config.R.

## Research Question

How well do simulation diagnostics behave when the population is already Normal?

## Population / Process and Theory

`X_i ~ N(0, 1)`. Because Normal distributions are closed under averaging, the sample mean is exactly `N(0, 1/n)` for every positive integer `n`, including `n = 1`.

## CLT Assumptions and Prediction

The observations are independent, identically distributed, and have finite variance. I expect all diagnostics to fluctuate around their theoretical targets at every tested `n`; those fluctuations quantify Monte Carlo noise.
